## Product (1–10)

**1. What is Slate in one sentence?**
A CR-run tool that lets one recorded schedule change (cancel/extra/move) reach every affected section instantly,
dated and attributed, instead of a WhatsApp poll.

**2. Who can make a change?**
Only a section's claimed CR (or an admin) — one CR per section, first student to claim it.

**3. Who decides who's a CR?**
Whoever claims it first, for a section that has none; admins can revoke.

**4. Do professors log in?**
No — explicitly a non-goal. Their names/timetable data only feed the slot-finder read-only.

**5. What are the three kinds of change?**
Cancelled, Extra, Moved (a linked cancel + extra sharing a `groupId`).

**6. What window of changes do students see?**
This week and next week only — enforced by `checkDate` on write and a DynamoDB TTL on read.

**7. What happens on Undo?**
The row is marked `undoneBy`/`undoneAt`, never deleted — history stays complete.

**8. Can a non-CR student see everything a CR sees?**
Everything except "Make a change" — same week view, same "My Batch" roster, same feed.

**9. What's a Batch vs a Section?**
Batch = program+branch+semester (e.g. B.Tech IT Sem 5); Section = A/B/C within it, with sub-groups B1/B2.

**10. What's an "effective timetable"?**
Regular classes that weekday, minus that date's cancellations, plus that date's extras — computed, never stored.

## Architecture / AWS (11–25)

**11. What's the stack, top to bottom?**
React + Vite/TS → Amplify Hosting → Cognito → AppSync (Amplify Gen 2 data) → DynamoDB → Lambda (5 functions).

**12. Which 5 Lambda functions exist?**
`pre-sign-up`, `parse-timetable`, `import-data`, `find-slots`, `section-changes`.

**13. What does the pre-sign-up Lambda do?**
Rejects any signup whose email domain isn't `@iiita.ac.in` — the closed-community boundary.

**14. What decides admin rights?**
Membership in the Cognito `ADMIN` group — not a `role` field on the User row, which is display-only.

**15. Where does authorization for CR actions live?**
Cedar, inside the `section-changes` Lambda, evaluating `policy.cedar` via `@cedar-policy/cedar-wasm`.

**16. Name Cedar's five permit rules.**
ClaimCr (own section, no CR yet), Cancel/AddExtra/Move (course's CRs), UndoGroup (maker), UndoSection (that
section's CR), and admin-can-do-anything.

**17. Where are Cedar decisions logged?**
CloudWatch, as structured JSON: `{cedar: decision, action, email, principal, resource}` — every call, allow or deny.

**18. What DB does everything live in?**
DynamoDB, via AppSync, generated by Amplify Gen 2's `a.schema()`.

**19. Why no RDS?**
Cost guardrail — no VPC/NAT/connection pooling needed; small dataset joined in Lambda memory instead of SQL.

**20. Why no OpenSearch/vector search anywhere?**
No similarity-matching problem exists here — slot-finding is exact interval math, not semantic ranking.

**21. What triggers ingestion?**
Admin uploads a spreadsheet to S3; `parse-timetable` reads it; admin reviews; `import-data` writes DynamoDB.

**22. Is PDF OCR (Textract/Bedrock) in the live path?**
No — `scripts/bedrock-normalize-timetable.py` exists but the live reader (`reader.ts`) is a spreadsheet parser.

**23. What notifies students of a change?**
Nothing pushed — an in-app "What changed" feed read on next load; SES was dropped from the plan.

**24. What scales to zero when idle?**
Everything — Lambda, DynamoDB on-demand, AppSync; no EC2/ECS/Fargate/EKS anywhere.

**25. What's the public deployment story?**
Amplify Hosting, CI from GitHub push, public URL live from Day 1.

## Data model (26–40)

**26. Name every table.**
User, TimetableSlot, ScheduleChange, ClassRep, RollRange, StudentSection, Enrollment.

**27. What's in a TimetableSlot row?**
program, branch, section, semester, day, startTime, endTime, courseId, room, faculty, sessionType, isElective.

**28. What does section `'*'` mean on a TimetableSlot?**
A whole-batch class — an elective basket, or a class the source sheet labels "All".

**29. What's `sessionType`?**
L (lecture) / P (practical) / T (tutorial), parsed from a tag in the source cell text.

**30. What does a ScheduleChange row's `kind` field hold?**
CANCELLED, EXTRA, MOVED_FROM, or MOVED_TO.

**31. How does a Move differ from a separate Cancel + Extra?**
Same `groupId` links the MOVED_FROM and MOVED_TO rows so Undo treats them as one action.

**32. What determines a student's section — their own choice?**
No — StudentSection (admin-uploaded list) or, as fallback, RollRange; never self-selected.

**33. What are Enrollment exceptions for?**
ADD (taking a course with another section/batch — drop-year, elective) or DROP (not taking a home-section course).

**34. Who writes StudentSection and Enrollment?**
Only the import-data Lambda / admin uploads — both ADMIN-group-only in the schema.

**35. What does ClassRep store?**
sectionKey, program/branch/semester/section, the Cognito `sub`, and email of the claiming student.

**36. Why is `sectionKey` derived from the main section only (B, not B1)?**
CRs are per main section — a B1/B2 class belongs to B's CR (see `keyOf` in the handler).

**37. What's `expiresAt` on ScheduleChange?**
A DynamoDB TTL: epoch seconds for the Monday after the change's week — auto-deletes old rows.

**38. What's `changedBySection` for?**
Attribution shown to students — which section's CR made this change, not just which email.

**39. Can a ScheduleChange row be edited after creation?**
No — only `undoneBy`/`undoneAt` are ever set via UpdateCommand; nothing else mutates.

**40. Where do faculty names come from?**
The course-legend table in the source spreadsheet, ingested into `TimetableSlot.faculty`.

## The slot-finding algorithm (41–52)

**41. Which Lambda runs it?**
`find-slots`.

**42. What are the "hard" constraints?**
Every affected section's effective timetable and the course professor's — a slot must be free for all of them.

**43. What are the "soft" constraints?**
Irregular attendees (enrollment-exception students) — clashing slots are still offered, ranked last, named.

**44. How are irregular students grouped for efficiency?**
By identical resulting timetable (a Set of slot ids joined into one key) — one Party per unique schedule.

**45. What ranking rules does it use?**
Avoids lunch (12:00–14:30), stays within 9:00–17:30, avoids sitting at the edge of any section's day.

**46. Is there any ML in the slot finder?**
None — explainable interval intersection plus a simple scoring function.

**47. What happens when no slot works for everyone?**
It reports which single section or the professor, if removed, would unlock the most slots, with a concrete example.

**48. How does room suggestion work?**
Every room seen in the ingested timetable, marked busy per overlapping class that date, lecture-use preferred.

**49. What's `blocks()` checking?**
Whether a TimetableSlot's section occupies part of, or all of, the section/sub-section being queried.

**50. Where does the professor's timetable come from for the slot search?**
Every TimetableSlot row with that `faculty` name, across every batch they teach — not just the CR's batch.

**51. Why intersect dates one weekday at a time instead of scanning a date range as one blob?**
Because busy sets are computed per calendar date (cancellations/extras are dated); a weekday's regular pattern
repeats but exceptions don't.

**52. Can two candidate slots tie in score?**
Yes — sorted by clash count, then score, then date, then start time, deterministically.

## Ingestion (53–60)

**53. What are the two ingestion steps?**
`parseTimetable` (read + validate, no writes) then `importData` (re-read + diff + write, with a `dryRun` option).

**54. Why two steps instead of one?**
So an admin reviews proposed rows/issues before anything touches DynamoDB — human-in-the-loop for messy sheets.

**55. What real-world quirks does the reader have to survive?**
Merged cells, cohort labels like "All", sub-sections (B1/B2) that vary by program.

**56. What happens when the reader can't confidently parse a row?**
It's surfaced as a validation issue for the admin, not silently guessed or dropped.

**57. Does `importData` overwrite existing rows blindly?**
No — it diffs against DynamoDB first; `removeMissing` is an explicit opt-in for deletions.

**58. What's `onlyPrefixes` for in importData?**
One sheet can list a whole admission year across programs (IIT/IIB/etc.); this filters which rolls belong to this import.

**59. Is ingestion accuracy proven or assumed?**
Explicitly unproven at spec time (CLAUDE.md §3) — hand-verification was the fallback plan if automated parsing failed.

**60. What's the honest admission about ingestion in the project brief?**
That accuracy on inconsistent institutional formatting was unknown until actually tested against real PDFs.
